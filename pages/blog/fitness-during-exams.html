Staying Fit During Exam Season

7 min read

Exam season is stressful. The temptation to skip the gym, live off energy drinks, and sit at a desk for 14 hours straight is real. But keeping active during exams isn't just good for your body — it's one of the most effective things you can do for your revision.

Why exercise helps your brain

Exercise increases blood flow to the brain, improves focus, reduces anxiety, and helps you sleep better. Studies consistently show that students who stay active during exams perform better than those who don't. Even 30 minutes of moderate exercise can boost cognitive function for several hours afterwards.

A realistic exam-season routine

You don't need to train like you normally would. The goal is maintenance, not progress. Here's a practical framework:

Option A: 3 sessions per week (45-60 mins)

  • Day 1: Upper body — push-ups, dumbbell press, rows, overhead press
  • Day 2: Lower body — squats, lunges, RDLs, leg press
  • Day 3: Full body — compound movements at moderate intensity

Option B: Daily movement (20-30 mins)

  • A brisk walk or jog between revision blocks
  • A bodyweight circuit at home (press-ups, squats, planks, burpees)
  • A short YouTube workout — no equipment needed

The best workout during exam season is the one you actually do. Perfection is the enemy of consistency.

Nutrition basics

You don't need a complex meal plan. Focus on three things:

  1. Eat enough protein: Chicken, eggs, Greek yoghurt, beans. Aim for a source at every meal.
  2. Stay hydrated: Keep a water bottle on your desk. Dehydration kills concentration.
  3. Don't skip meals: Your brain runs on glucose. Skipping breakfast to "save time" costs you more in lost focus than it saves.

Sleep is non-negotiable

Pulling all-nighters feels productive but destroys retention. Your brain consolidates memories during sleep — without it, the revision you did barely sticks. Aim for 7-8 hours, and try to keep a consistent sleep schedule even during exam weeks.

Putting it together

A good exam day might look like this:

  • 7:30am: Wake up, breakfast, quick stretch
  • 8:30 - 12:00: Focused revision (Pomodoro blocks)
  • 12:00 - 1:00: Lunch and a 20-minute walk
  • 1:00 - 4:00: Afternoon revision
  • 4:00 - 5:00: Gym or home workout
  • 5:30 - 7:30: Final revision block
  • 8:00pm: Dinner, wind down, early night

The key is making exercise a non-negotiable part of your day — just like revision. You'll feel better, think clearer, and perform better in your exams.
