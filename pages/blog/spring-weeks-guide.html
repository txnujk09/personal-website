Spring Weeks

The Complete Guide to Spring Weeks

10 min read

Spring weeks (also called spring insight programmes or spring internships) are short work experience programmes — usually one to two weeks — offered by banks, consulting firms, law firms, and tech companies. They're aimed at first-year university students (or penultimate-year students in Scotland), and they're one of the best ways to get your foot in the door for summer internships and graduate roles.

Why spring weeks matter

Most top firms use spring weeks as a direct pipeline to their summer internship programmes. Perform well during the week, and you'll often receive a fast-tracked interview — or even a direct offer — for a summer placement. This means one week of effort in your first year can set you up for the next three years of your career.

When to apply

Applications typically open between September and November for programmes running the following March or April. The key rule: apply early. Many firms use rolling admissions, meaning they fill spots as applications come in. Waiting until the deadline often means competing for fewer places.

A rough timeline

  • September - October: Research firms and programmes. Start drafting your CV.
  • October - November: Submit applications as they open. Don't wait for "the perfect CV."
  • November - January: Online tests and video interviews for shortlisted candidates.
  • February - March: Assessment centres and final decisions.
  • March - April: The programmes themselves.

How to find programmes

Start with these approaches:

  1. Company websites: Go directly to the early careers pages of firms you're interested in.
  2. University careers services: Your careers team will have listings and can review your applications.
  3. Student forums and spreadsheets: Communities on Reddit and The Student Room maintain shared spreadsheets tracking open programmes and deadlines.
  4. LinkedIn: Follow early careers accounts of target firms for announcements.

The application itself

Most applications involve some combination of:

  • A CV (one page, clean formatting, no photo)
  • Competency questions ("Tell us about a time you showed leadership...")
  • A cover letter or motivational statement
  • Online psychometric or numerical tests

The number one mistake I see is generic answers. Every answer should show why you want this specific firm and this specific division. Do your research.

Interview and assessment centre tips

If you make it past the application stage, you'll likely face a video interview or assessment centre. Here's what helps:

  • Know your CV: Be ready to discuss everything on it in detail.
  • Prepare commercial awareness: Read the Financial Times or Bloomberg for a few weeks before. Know what's happening in markets.
  • Practice with friends: Mock interviews make a massive difference. Practice answering out loud, not just in your head.
  • Be yourself: Firms are looking for people they'd want to work with. Be genuine, curious, and engaged.

Final thoughts

Spring weeks are competitive, but they're far more accessible than most people think. The majority of applicants don't prepare properly — if you put in the work on your applications, research the firms, and practice your interview technique, you'll already be ahead of most candidates.
