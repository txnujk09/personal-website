A Student's Guide to Starting at the Gym

8 min read

Starting at the gym can feel intimidating — especially when everyone else seems to know what they're doing. The truth is, most people felt exactly the same when they started. Here's the no-nonsense guide I wish I'd had.

Choosing a gym

Don't overthink it. Pick the gym that's closest to you or on your daily route. The best gym is the one you'll actually go to consistently. Budget options like PureGym and The Gym Group are perfectly fine — you don't need a fancy facility to make progress.

Your first programme

As a beginner, you don't need a complex split routine. A simple full-body workout 3 times per week is the most effective way to build a foundation. Here's a starter programme:

Full Body A

  • Barbell squat — 3 sets of 8
  • Bench press — 3 sets of 8
  • Barbell row — 3 sets of 8
  • Overhead press — 3 sets of 10
  • Plank — 3 sets of 30 seconds

Full Body B

  • Romanian deadlift — 3 sets of 8
  • Incline dumbbell press — 3 sets of 10
  • Lat pulldown — 3 sets of 10
  • Leg press — 3 sets of 10
  • Face pulls — 3 sets of 15

Alternate between A and B, training Monday/Wednesday/Friday (or any three non-consecutive days). Focus on learning proper form before adding weight.

Nutrition on a student budget

You don't need expensive supplements or meal prep containers. Here are budget-friendly high-protein options:

  • Eggs: Cheap, versatile, high quality protein
  • Chicken thighs: Cheaper than breast, more flavour
  • Greek yoghurt: High protein, great snack
  • Tinned tuna/beans: Shelf-stable, cheap, nutritious
  • Oats: Cheap carbs, filling, versatile
  • Frozen vegetables: Cheaper than fresh, just as nutritious

You don't need to eat perfectly. You need to eat enough protein (roughly 1.6-2g per kg of bodyweight) and enough calories to fuel your training. Everything else is detail.

Common beginner mistakes

  1. Ego lifting: Using weight that's too heavy with terrible form. Check your ego at the door and lift what you can control.
  2. Programme hopping: Switching routines every two weeks because you're not seeing results. Stick to one programme for at least 8-12 weeks.
  3. Skipping legs: Just don't.
  4. Ignoring recovery: Progress happens when you rest, not when you train. Sleep 7-8 hours and take rest days seriously.
  5. Overcomplicating it: You don't need pre-workout, BCAAs, creatine, or a specific meal timing strategy. Get the basics right first.

Staying consistent

Consistency beats intensity. Three average sessions per week for a year will give you better results than six intense sessions for two weeks followed by quitting. Build the habit first — the results will follow.

Tips for staying consistent:

  • Go at the same time each day — make it routine
  • Track your workouts in a notes app or journal
  • Find a gym partner for accountability
  • Remember that showing up on days you don't feel like it is what separates people who get results from people who don't
